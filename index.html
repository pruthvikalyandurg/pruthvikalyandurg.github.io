Scientist · Entrepreneur · Photographer

Nature already holds the answers. The work is to decode them, and build.

I'm Pruthvi Kalyandurg, a molecular biologist in Uppsala working on RNA biology and plant health, and co-founder of a biotech startup that builds with biology instead of toxic chemicals.

01About

From understanding biological systems to building with them.

i.

Where it began

I grew up in Anantapur, one of the driest districts in India. That is where my interest in plants began, and improving their tolerance to stress has driven my work ever since.

I studied biotechnology in Tamil Nadu, and then spent two years volunteering with Landmark Worldwide, a global personal development training organisation, as an instructional lead and production supervisor for their courses. Alongside that, I volunteered with Lok Satta, a political party working for clean politics in India. Those years taught me a great deal about people, communication and leading teams.

ii.

Understanding biology

When I returned to science, I knew I wanted to study plants, and there was no better place for that than Uppsala, the city of Linnaeus. I came to Uppsala University for my master's, stayed on for a PhD at SLU, and found my field: plants, the viruses that infect them, and the long contest between the two.

iii.

Building with biology

What has stayed with me through years of research is how capable plants already are of protecting themselves. Nature has spent millions of years refining those defences. I believe the future of agriculture lies in learning from them, working with biology rather than relying on toxic chemicals, and that is what I am building towards with my startup today.

02Research

What I have worked on

  1. Plants and fungi can recognise a threat and respond with remarkable precision. I study how that built-in defence works, and how it can protect a plant without leaving any chemical behind.

  2. I spent years working out how plant viruses spread from cell to cell, why they change so quickly, and the tricks they use to switch off a plant's immune response.

  3. A plant is always trading signals with the microbes around it, some harmful and some helpful. I read that exchange to see what keeps a plant healthy and what makes it sick.

  4. Some fungi protect plants by keeping disease in check. I study how they do it, and how to make that protection dependable enough to use on a real farm.

  5. Good lab results rarely reach a farmer on their own. My work closes that gap, taking a proven idea and turning it into something that works at scale.
